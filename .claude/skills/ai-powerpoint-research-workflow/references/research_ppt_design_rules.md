# Research PPT Design Rules

## Core philosophy

A research presentation slide is a **visual claim**. Each slide answers one question: "What one thing should the audience remember from this page?"

## Typography

- English: Arial, Helvetica, Calibri, or Times New Roman
- Chinese: Microsoft YaHei, STHeiti, or template default
- Title: **≥ 28 pt** (Chinese), **≥ 24 pt** (English)
- Body: **≥ 18 pt** (Chinese), **≥ 16 pt** (English)
- Figure labels: **≥ 12 pt**, readable when projected
- Maximum font variations per deck: **2** (one heading face, one body face)

## Color

- Background: white or very light gray
- Text: dark gray or black
- Accent: one unified color for the entire deck
- Figure colors: restrained academic palettes (avoid neon, excessive gradients)
- Table headers: **bold only** — no dark blue backgrounds unless the template requires it

## Layout

- Aspect ratio: **16:9**
- Content area: leave margins on all four sides
- One message per slide: no exceptions
- Maximum **5 bullet points** per slide
- Maximum **2 levels** of hierarchy
- Figures should occupy at least 40% of the slide for results pages
- Place the conclusion at the top or in the title, not at the bottom

## Figures in PPT

- All figures must have a source note (bottom or in the slide notes)
- Screenshots of tables from papers: must cite the source
- Do not stretch figures disproportionally
- Do not use AI-generated images as real research data
- Charts must have axis labels, units, and legible legends

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it is bad | Fix |
|---------|---------------|-----|
| Pasting Word paragraphs | Audience cannot read and listen at the same time | Extract 3 core bullet points |
| Title is a section name ("Results") | Does not communicate anything | Use a conclusion as the title |
| Too many decimal places | Hard to read during a talk | Round to 2-3 significant figures |
| Figure legends too small | Invisible on a projector | Increase to ≥12 pt |
| Dark backgrounds on data slides | Hard to read, wastes ink | Use white or light background |
| Using "we can see that" in slide text | Spoken language in written form | Delete and let the speaker say it |
